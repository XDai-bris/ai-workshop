Stage 4

At a milestone: a new reviewer with new questions

28–35 min

At a milestone, bring in a reviewer that did not build the work, ideally a different model. Give it the questions the earlier checks could not answer: recompute without the code, test the tests, and ask whether each number is a plausible size.

After Stage 3 the project looks finished. It has a fix, a passing test, two commits and a report with tight intervals, and it is about to go to the group. We sent one milestone prompt to a different model, OpenAI’s gpt-6.2-astra in the Codex app, and, for comparison, to Claude Sonnet 5.5 in three fresh sessions.

Run 1 is worth a minute. It asked exactly the right question about the test, and answered it with the same misreading that caused the error. It noticed that the estimate was several times too large, and explained it away. Seeing the anomaly is not enough; something has to make the reviewer follow it up.

What made the difference

The same model, Claude Sonnet 5.5, found E1 in 1 of 5 runs with the Stage 2 prompt and in 2 of 3 with this one, each time recomputing 0.207 and pointing at the test’s THETA * bx_sd / SD_BMI. The new questions did much of the work. Each targets something Stage 2 exposed: recompute without the script, because reviewers trusted it; ask whether the test shares an assumption, because the simulations did; ask about plausibility, because nobody had. The second model found E1 in both of its runs. Both runs also spelled out a consequence the Claude runs only touched on: Q does not change when every exposure effect is multiplied by a constant. The diagnostics that made the Stage 2 result look coherent could never have caught this error. Two runs of one model and three of another are small samples, and we do not claim more than that.

Acting on the review: verify first, then fix

A reviewer can be wrong too. So the next prompt asks the agent to check the finding before changing anything, and to give the test an answer worked out by hand.

The independent test immediately exposed something real: a property of MR-Egger, not a bug. The agent was right to stop and ask rather than widen the tolerance, because that judgement belongs to the researcher.

What the failing check was, and the researcher’s decisionEgger regression dilution · Prompt 6

With a known answer that no longer shared the code’s assumption, the outcome effects were large relative to their noise, so the test became much more precise. We ran the test’s design on 1,000 simulated datasets: the mean MR-Egger slope was 1.92, the regression dilution MR-Egger shows when exposure effects are measured with error (I²GX ≈ 0.96; Bowden et al. 2016). One detail: the reply says its own multi-seed simulation is in scratch/egger_sim.R. That file does not exist; the sandbox had blocked the command that would have written it.
